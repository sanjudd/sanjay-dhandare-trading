Step one

A single line, four different messages

The 9 EMA reacts fast, so it reports momentum almost in real time. Read which of these four states you are in before you look at anything else.

Bullish momentum

Price holds above a rising 9 EMA. Pullbacks get bought.

Bearish momentum

Price stays under a falling 9 EMA. Rallies get sold.

Choppy — stand aside

Price cuts through the line again and again. No edge here.

Extended — don't chase

Price runs far from the line. Wait for it to come back.

Step two

Mark your levels before the market moves

Levels drawn in a calm mind are worth more than levels drawn mid-trade. Five sources cover almost every intraday decision.

  • Previous day's high, low and close The levels every other desk is watching too.
  • Major consolidation zones Where price spent time, it will react again.
  • Today's opening range The first 15 minutes set the day's boundaries.
  • Major swing high and low The turning points that produced the current leg.
  • Strong rejection zones Long wicks mark where size was waiting.

Don't redraw a level for every small candle. A level you move is a level you never had.

Step three

Five checks, always in this order

Skip one and the next stops meaning anything. Scroll through and watch the sequence light up.

1

Identify the trend

Price on one side of the 9 EMA, and the line sloping the same way. If those disagree, there is no trade to look for.

2

Find the S/R zone

A level you marked earlier has to be within reach. A good candle at a random price is still a random trade.

3

Wait for the pullback

Let price come back to the 9 EMA and the level together. You are paid for waiting, not for being early.

4

Look for the rejection

A long wick, a hard reversal close — proof that the level was defended by real orders.

5

Enter on confirmation

Only when the next candle breaks the rejection candle. The market has now put a price on its own opinion.
