Three questions, in order

If any one of them is a no, there is no trade. Most days the answer is no for hours at a time, and that is the job.

01 — trend

Which way

Price on one side of the 9 EMA, and the line sloping the same way. When those two disagree, the chart is telling me to sit out.

02 — location

Where

A level I marked before 9:15 has to be within reach. A clean candle at a price nobody cares about is still a coin flip.

03 — proof

Says who

A rejection candle at the level, then a candle that breaks it. Only then has the market put its own money behind the idea.

The part that actually decides the year

The entry rules fit on a napkin. Everything that makes them work happens away from the chart.

  • DisciplineThe setup is the setup. A beautiful candle at the wrong level is still a pass.
  • RoutineLevels marked before the open. Same checklist, same order, every morning.
  • EducationBacktest before risking anything. The market charges tuition either way.
  • ActionSize it, place the stop, then leave it alone until it resolves.
  • MentalityA loss inside the plan is rent. A win outside it is a problem I will pay for later.

    Rules I do not negotiate with

    Written down when the market was closed, which is the only time anyone thinks clearly about risk.

    • 01Nothing in the first fifteen minutes. Let the range form.
    • 02Three trades a day, maximum. Not three good ones — three.
    • 03Risk per trade is fixed before the day starts and never revised upward.
    • 04No averaging down. The stop is where I was wrong, not a suggestion.
    • 05If the 9 EMA is flat and price keeps crossing it, the day is finished.
    • 06Flat before 3:10. I do not hold intraday positions into the close.
    • 07Two losses in a row and the terminal closes.
    • 08Every trade goes in the journal the same evening, including the ones I regret.
